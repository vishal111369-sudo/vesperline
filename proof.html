How we prove results

Most reports show what happened after the work. We show what happened because of it, in your own data, with a method your finance team can check.

Why most reports flatter the work

Most tools use attribution (giving a campaign credit for anything a customer did after receiving it). The trouble is that many of those customers would have bought, returned or renewed anyway.

A simple exampleSpend per customer
Customers who received the offer40
Similar customers who did not30
What the offer actually added10

An attribution report would credit the offer with 40. The real effect was 10. Decisions taken on the first number waste budget on customers who never needed the push.

Our method

  • A holdout group (a small random group, usually 5 to 10%) gets none of the new work for an agreed period. It shows what would have happened anyway.
  • Every larger campaign also gets its own control group (customers picked at random who skip that one campaign).
  • We count everyone who was picked, not only the people who opened a message, so engaged customers don't flatter the result.
  • We measure net revenue over 30 days, after every cost including rewards and discounts, and check the weeks after for spend that was only moved earlier.
  • Big spenders are spread evenly across the groups, so one large customer can't decide the result by luck.
